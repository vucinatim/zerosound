# ZeroSound v0.11 Reliable Transport and Continuous Sync

Status: implementation, automated verification, and OTA publication complete; physical office
validation pending

Target: v0.11

Scope: replace the shared UDP control/audio transport and complete continuous room-clock playout
reconciliation

## Goal

ZeroSound must keep two to eight Macs synchronized through ordinary Wi-Fi loss, clock drift, members
joining or leaving, transient stalls, reconnects, and coordinator replacement. A user must never need
to leave and rejoin a room to restore synchronization.

This is a transport and timing architecture change, not a retry patch. Room discovery, room domain,
UI concepts, capture, and the compact PCM audio packet remain where they are already sound. The
shared UDP control/audio connection and original-anchor-only playout discipline are replaced cleanly,
and the obsolete paths are deleted before release.

## Product contract

- Joining either succeeds with an explicit acknowledgement or fails with a useful timeout and retry
  action. It never spins forever.
- A non-source member joining or leaving cannot interrupt, restart, or move the phase of existing
  renderers.
- A missing or late audio packet may be concealed, but cannot permanently shift playback.
- Clock estimates continue improving while audio plays, and every renderer follows the current room
  clock rather than its initial local anchor.
- A recoverable network or renderer discontinuity returns to room phase automatically with a short
  fade. Manual leave/rejoin is not a recovery mechanism.
- Coordinator replacement preserves the room and converges without split-brain state. If live audio
  must restart, it does so once against a fresh scheduled anchor.
- Diagnostics distinguish control connectivity, audio delivery, clock lock, buffer health, hardware
  underruns, and room-phase correction.

## Locked architecture

### Discovery

Bonjour/mDNS remains the passive discovery mechanism. It advertises only small room metadata and the
current coordinator endpoint. Audio is never multicast or broadcast over the LAN.

The v0.11 service is a TCP Bonjour service. Its TXT record includes the room identity, coordinator
identity and term, protocol versions, member/source summary, and UDP audio port. Discovery remains
read-only and owns no room membership state.

### Reliable control plane

All state-changing or state-reconstructing traffic uses one ordered TCP control channel per member:

- hello, compatibility negotiation, join acceptance/rejection, and leave;
- authoritative snapshots and membership changes;
- source assignment, start, stop, and generation changes;
- coordinator term and recovery state;
- heartbeats, health telemetry, errors, and explicit acknowledgements.

TCP messages use a small length-prefixed frame codec with a strict maximum frame size. The codec must
correctly handle partial reads, multiple frames in one read, malformed lengths, and connection close
mid-frame. Network.framework connection callbacks are wrapped by a focused `ControlChannel`; room
domain code never parses stream bytes.

Join has an explicit state machine and deadline:

1. Establish TCP.
2. Send a versioned join request.
3. Receive join acceptance with the authoritative snapshot and a short-lived UDP registration token.
4. Register the UDP audio path.
5. Receive audio-path readiness.
6. Enter the room and begin speaker priming when a stream is live.

Failure at any step closes both transports, removes partial coordinator state, and reports a bounded
error. Reconnect uses a new connection epoch so callbacks from an older attempt cannot mutate the
current room.

### UDP audio and timing plane

Audio remains compact UDP unicast. For a room of eight Macs, the coordinator encodes each packet once
and sends the same datagram to seven peers. The existing 240-frame stereo PCM packet is approximately
1,008 payload bytes, avoids IP fragmentation, and uses about 1.6 Mbit/s per receiver. This is the
correct reliability/complexity tradeoff for the trusted-office scope.

Each accepted TCP member registers a dedicated UDP peer using the token issued during join. The UDP
plane carries only:

- audio packets;
- registration and minimal liveness datagrams;
- sequence-numbered clock ping/pong samples, which remain loss-tolerant and avoid TCP
  head-of-line-delay bias.

The audio router has a dedicated concurrency owner and bounded per-peer outbound work. It drops stale
audio rather than allowing latency to grow. Control events never share the audio socket or its serial
work queue. A slow receiver cannot delay another receiver or the coordinator reducer.

### Coordinator ownership

The coordinator session composes three independent components:

```text
RoomCoordinatorSession
  RoomStateMachine       authoritative room decisions
  ControlServer          reliable member channels
  UDPAudioRouter         real-time packet and clock routing
```

The session coordinates their lifecycle but does not merge their responsibilities. Member identity is
bound to one control connection epoch and one registered UDP endpoint. Stale endpoints, callbacks,
terms, registrations, and source generations are rejected explicitly.

A newly authoritative snapshot can demote a former coordinator. Bonjour deduplication selects the
highest coordinator term deterministically and never chooses an arbitrary equal-term endpoint.

## Continuous synchronization model

### One room timeline

Every audio packet identifies a sample range on the coordinator room timeline. Every receiver owns an
affine room-clock mapping:

```text
room time = local monotonic time * clock rate + clock offset
```

Clock samples update this mapping continuously without discontinuously jumping it. The source uses the
mapping to timestamp captured audio. Receivers invert the current mapping to determine when each audio
sample should leave their output device.

### Stream timeline

The jitter buffer preserves packet presentation timestamps as the authority for the stream timeline.
Sequence numbers determine ordering and loss; nominal frame duration is used to validate continuity
and synthesize missing audio, not to permanently replace incoming timing evidence.

A `StreamTimeline` maps player sample positions to room presentation time. It survives ordinary packet
loss but resets on a fenced source generation, sample-format change, or explicit discontinuity.

### Playout discipline

The renderer measures two separate facts:

1. hardware phase: the AVAudioPlayerNode playhead and output latency;
2. room phase: the hardware presentation time compared with the current room-clock conversion of the
   corresponding stream sample.

A focused `PlayoutDisciplineController` consumes filtered room-phase error and buffer depth:

- errors inside the deadband keep nominal rate;
- small sustained errors receive bounded, inaudible varispeed correction;
- buffer pressure may bias that correction within the same bound;
- a persistent discontinuity schedules one fresh future room-time anchor and crossfades;
- loss concealment advances exactly the missing sample count and never changes phase.

Hard re-anchoring must use fresh packet room timestamps converted through the latest clock model. It
must not reuse a scheduled timestamp derived only from the old local anchor.

### Transition isolation

Joining, leaving, renaming, telemetry, and room advertisement changes do not touch stream generation,
jitter state, AVAudioEngine state, or the playback timeline. Only these events may reset playout:

- source or stream-generation change;
- sample-format change;
- output-device/engine configuration change;
- detected unrecoverable timeline discontinuity;
- explicit room exit.

This invariant is enforced in domain tests and full transport/timing simulations.

## Implementation phases

### Phase 0 — Characterization and invariants

- Capture current packet size/rate, queue depth, CPU, memory, clock skew, and renderer phase.
- Add a deterministic end-to-end timing simulation with separate coordinator, host, and audio-hardware
  clocks.
- Reproduce permanent original-anchor drift, burst starvation, and join/leave during playback in tests.
- Record the current real-network failure modes as failing acceptance tests.

Exit condition: the known failures are reproducible without relying on an office-wide manual test.

### Phase 1 — Pure timing core

- Introduce `StreamTimeline` and make packet room timestamps authoritative throughout playback.
- Replace original-anchor phase measurement with current room-phase measurement.
- Separate clock estimation, stream mapping, hardware observation, and discipline decisions.
- Implement soft correction, buffer-pressure correction, and fresh-timestamp hard re-anchoring as pure,
  deterministic policies.
- Preserve fades and packet-loss concealment without allowing either to shift the timeline.

Exit condition: simulated clocks with independent skew converge and remain bounded through loss,
jitter, estimator updates, and burst stalls.

### Phase 2 — Framed TCP control

- Implement and fuzz the bounded frame codec.
- Implement control client/server connection lifecycle and epochs.
- Move joins, snapshots, commands, source state, coordinator state, heartbeat, and telemetry to TCP.
- Add join acknowledgement, deadline, rejection, cancellation, and stale-attempt cleanup.

Exit condition: control state converges under arbitrary stream read boundaries and never depends on
UDP delivery.

### Phase 3 — Dedicated UDP audio router

- Add authenticated per-session UDP registration.
- Move audio and clock sampling onto the dedicated datagram plane.
- Add bounded per-peer sends, stale-packet dropping, endpoint cleanup, and packet-stage metrics.
- Prove that one slow or disappearing receiver cannot stall other peers.

Exit condition: eight clients can exchange reliable control while the audio plane experiences loss,
reordering, duplication, and stalls independently.

### Phase 4 — Session integration and legacy deletion

- Compose the new control server, UDP router, and reducer in `RoomCoordinatorSession`.
- Replace the old `RoomCoordinator` and `RoomConnection` orchestration atomically.
- Make coordinator promotion and demotion explicit and epoch-fenced.
- Change Bonjour to the v0.11 TCP service and reject older protocol versions clearly.
- Delete the shared UDP control/audio implementation and all compatibility adapters.

Exit condition: there is one production transport path and no dormant fallback architecture.

### Phase 5 — Diagnostics and fault verification

- Report control state, join stage/age, UDP registration, packet arrival/loss/late/reorder counts,
  buffer depth, clock lock/skew, room-phase error, playback correction, underruns, and re-anchor reason.
- Keep user-facing health calm and base it on recent actionable failures rather than lifetime counters.
- Run deterministic randomized fault tests with recorded seeds.
- Run Thread Sanitizer and repeated create/join/leave/source/coordinator cycles.

Exit condition: every recovery is explainable from diagnostics and all fault suites are repeatable.

### Phase 6 — Real-network validation and release

- Test two, five, and eight physical Macs on normal and congested office Wi-Fi.
- Exercise source/non-source joins and leaves, coordinator exit, sleep/wake, output-device changes, and
  temporary Wi-Fi interruption during live audio.
- Run multi-hour mixed-hardware soak tests and measure phase rather than judging only by ear.
- Profile coordinator CPU, memory, send backlog, and aggregate network traffic.
- Publish one protocol-breaking v0.11 update and verify the unsigned Sparkle update path from the
  previous production build.

Exit condition: the acceptance criteria pass on deterministic tests and the physical office setup.

## Automated test matrix

### Control transport

- split and coalesced TCP frames;
- invalid, oversized, truncated, and incompatible frames;
- lost connection before and after join acceptance;
- join timeout and retry with no duplicate membership;
- stale callbacks from a superseded connection epoch;
- eight simultaneous joins and orderly/random disconnects;
- coordinator promotion, demotion, equal-term conflict, and stale-term rejection.

### UDP audio transport

- registration token acceptance, replay rejection, and expiry;
- independent loss, reordering, duplication, and burst delay;
- bounded fan-out with one slow receiver;
- stale generation/source/endpoint rejection;
- packet-size and no-fragmentation guard;
- control remains responsive while audio is saturated.

### Synchronization

- host clocks and output clocks with independent positive/negative skew;
- changing clock estimates during uninterrupted playback;
- one packet, burst, and reordered loss without phase shift;
- buffer underflow and automatic recovery;
- abrupt room-clock correction and fresh re-anchor;
- output-engine configuration change;
- non-source member joins/leaves during playback without timeline mutation;
- source transfer and coordinator replacement with generation fencing;
- multi-hour virtual soak with deterministic randomized faults.

## Acceptance criteria

- A healthy join receives an explicit acceptance and registered audio path within two seconds.
- An unreachable or incompatible room exits joining with a useful error within five seconds.
- Existing renderers do not restart and their measured software phase changes by less than 1 ms when a
  non-source member joins or leaves.
- After convergence on a healthy LAN, absolute software room-phase error remains below 2 ms under
  normal clock skew and jitter.
- A recoverable burst stall returns below 5 ms phase error within three seconds without user action.
- Packet loss and concealment never create a permanent sample-count or timeline offset.
- No renderer can remain more than 10 ms out of room phase indefinitely; it must converge or perform
  one controlled fresh re-anchor.
- Eight-member PCM fan-out remains bounded with no unbounded queue, increasing latency, or control
  starvation.
- Coordinator replacement never creates two visible endpoints for the same highest term and never
  requires manual leave/rejoin.
- All deterministic, integration, sanitizer, release, and physical-office checks pass before v0.11 is
  distributed.

These are software-timeline targets. Physical sound arrival also varies by listener/speaker distance
at roughly 3 ms per metre and is outside this non-spatial product's correction model.

## Explicit non-goals

- multicast or broadcast audio;
- Opus, forward-error correction, or adaptive bitrate without measured need;
- true surround channels or physical speaker-position compensation;
- WAN rooms, relay servers, or internet traversal;
- generic distributed consensus;
- compatibility inside one room between the old shared-UDP protocol and v0.11;
- redesigning the approved single-room UI.

## Definition of done

The goal is complete only when:

- TCP owns every reliable room operation and UDP owns only loss-tolerant audio/timing traffic;
- joins have explicit acknowledgement, timeout, cancellation, and clean retry;
- the renderer continuously follows the current room clock and packet stream timeline;
- ordinary membership changes cannot mutate active playback;
- every discontinuity either converges smoothly or performs one fresh controlled re-anchor;
- coordinator handoff and stale endpoint cleanup are deterministic;
- the old shared UDP connection and original-anchor-only correction path are deleted;
- diagnostics identify the stage and reason for every degraded or recovering state;
- automated fault tests and physical two/five/eight-Mac validation meet the acceptance criteria;
- one verified universal production archive and update feed are published.
